# Customer discovery interview guide

Purpose: learn how recurring imagery-based construction or urban-change monitoring is
performed and purchased. Do not demo or pitch RSDiff during discovery.

## Recruiting screen

Interview people who personally performed, reviewed, commissioned, or purchased at
least one imagery-based monitoring job in the last 18 months. Record role and decision
involvement. Exclude general AI enthusiasts without a completed workflow.

Suggested invitation:

> I am researching how GIS teams monitor construction and urban change from repeated
> imagery. I am not selling software. Could I ask about the last project you completed,
> its workflow, and deliverables in a 25-minute call? I will share an anonymized summary.

## Questions

1. Tell me about the last project where you compared imagery across dates. What decision
   triggered the work?
2. Who requested it, who performed it, and who approved the result?
3. What imagery and supporting layers did you receive? Who purchased or licensed them?
4. Walk through the work from receiving imagery to delivering the result.
5. Which step consumed the most analyst time? How long did the entire job take?
6. What qualified as a relevant change? What had to be ignored?
7. Where did registration, seasonal differences, shadows, resolution, or cloud cover
   cause errors?
8. What was delivered: polygons, raster, spreadsheet, dashboard, PDF, or another format?
9. How were detections reviewed and corrected? What evidence was required for approval?
10. Could imagery leave your network? What retention, hosting, or audit rules applied?
11. What software or external services were used, and what was unsatisfactory?
12. Was this one-off or recurring? How many sites, square kilometres, or updates per year?
13. What did the workflow cost in staff time, imagery, contractors, and software?
14. Who could approve a pilot or purchase, and what procurement threshold/process applies?
15. If the same result arrived faster, what measurable outcome would improve?
16. May we follow up with a sanitized example, and who else understands the buying side?

Avoid hypothetical feature questions and “would you use/pay?” questions. Ask for past
behavior, artifacts, quantities, and introductions.

## Evidence captured per interview

- completed-job date and vertical;
- recurring frequency and scale;
- user, approver, and economic buyer;
- input imagery/provider and rights constraints;
- workflow steps, tools, duration, and cost;
- required output and acceptance process;
- top three failure modes;
- network/privacy requirements;
- sanitized-data commitment;
- buyer introduction or pilot commitment.

## Scorecard

Score each 0–2:

| Criterion | 0 | 1 | 2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Recurrence | One-off | Occasional | Monthly/quarterly or many sites |
| Pain | Mild inconvenience | Material delay | Missed obligation/revenue/risk |
| Quantified cost | Unknown | Time or cost estimated | Both documented |
| Existing spend | None | Staff/tools | External contract or budget owner |
| Data access | Cannot share/test | Public proxy possible | Sanitized real example offered |
| Reach to buyer | Unknown | Buyer identified | Introduction offered |
| Security fit | Requirements unknown | Cloud or local feasible | On-prem need matches hypothesis |
| Pilot intent | Polite interest | Follow-up agreed | Concrete pilot/data/date |

Strong interview: at least 11/16 with no zero for data access or reach to buyer.

## Phase gate

Continue to a concierge pilot only when five interviews describe the same recurring job,
three quantify cost, three provide sanitized examples, two identify or introduce the
buyer, and one accepts a manual or paid pilot.
